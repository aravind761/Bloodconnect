Clinical Transfusion Guidelines & FAQs

Why is O negative considered the Universal Red Cell donor?

O negative red blood cells contain neither A nor B glycoproteins on their surface membranes, and they lack the Rh factor (D antigen). As a result, when transfused into an emergency patient, the recipient's immune system will not identify the red cells as foreign, avoiding acute hemolytic transfusion reactions.

Why is plasma compatibility reversed compared to red blood cells?

In red cell donation, we avoid donor antigens that the recipient's antibodies could attack. In plasma donation, we are transferring donor antibodies! Since AB individuals have neither anti-A nor anti-B antibodies in their plasma, AB plasma is the universal plasma donor and safe for all patients.

How frequently can someone donate platelets versus whole blood?

While whole blood requires 90 to 120 days for red cells and iron stores to fully reconstitute, platelets are replenished by the spleen and bone marrow within 72 hours. Apheresis donors can safely give platelets every 7 to 14 days, up to 24 times in a calendar year.
